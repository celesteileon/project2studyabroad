A note from me

Curious by nature. A storyteller by habit.

I'm Celeste, a University of San Francisco student studying Advertising with a concentration in Creativity and minors in Design and Public Relations. I am drawn to the way people tell stories — through images, places, design, food, and the small details that make an experience feel personal. I love creative work that connects people and gives them a reason to care.

During Spring 2026, I spent five months based in Madrid. I arrived with a long list of places I wanted to see and left with something more meaningful than a checklist: familiar streets, favorite coffee shops, dinners that became weekly traditions, and the confidence that comes from navigating a new place on your own. Weekends took me across Europe and to Morocco, but Madrid was the constant that made those adventures feel connected.

Travel taught me that being somewhere new is not only about seeing the famous landmarks. It is also about learning how to get groceries, finding a café you return to, figuring out the metro, keeping a journal, and letting a city become ordinary in the best possible way. This site is my scrapbook and my attempt to pass along the notes I wish I had at the beginning.

Before you go: my Madrid guide

My biggest recommendation is to join City Life Madrid events and trips whenever you can. I took cooking classes and trips with them and had a great experience. If you book their Morocco trip, the guide Adam was fantastic.

Know before you go

Practical tips, learned the real way.

TIP 01 / PLANNING

Travel Thursday to Monday

When your class schedule allows, leaving Thursday night and returning Monday can make a weekend feel much longer. Confirm class obligations and transport before booking.

TIP 02 / EXPERIENCES

Say yes to organized events

City Life Madrid was one of my favorite resources for cooking classes and trips. It can be an easy way to meet people and explore without planning every detail yourself.

TIP 03 / SOUVENIRS

Collect paper, not clutter

Postcards were my favorite souvenir. I aimed for at least two per weekend and kept tickets and other paper memorabilia for a collage. A magnet from each location is fun, too. Skip souvenirs you will never use.

TIP 04 / MEMORY KEEPING

Keep a journal

Write down the restaurant, the funny moment, the street name, or the detail you think you will remember forever. You might not — and the notes become priceless later.

TIP 05 / BOOKINGS

Compare the whole journey

I used Omio, Booking.com, and Skyscanner. I checked Wizz Air, easyJet, and Ryanair for flights; pay close attention to luggage rules and airport locations before choosing the cheapest fare.

TIP 06 / GETTING AROUND

Use the city transport mix

Madrid's metro is useful for everyday travel. Bolt was my go-to rideshare, with Uber, Cabify, and FreeNow also options. Download apps and check payment details before you need them.

TIP 07 / EVERYDAY LIFE

Learn the local basics

For groceries, I liked Carrefour Market; Dia and Mercadona were other options. TEDi was useful for home items. Glovo is a common food delivery option in Spain.

TIP 08 / PACKING

Pack for real life

Bring layers, comfortable walking shoes, and outfits you can repeat. Check the linked packing list before departure, and leave room in your bag for the things you bring home.
