March 31 through April 4Italy · S tier

Lake Como: the trip I still replay in my head

There are places that are beautiful in photographs, and then there are places that make you put your phone away because the real view is better. Lake Como was that kind of place for me. The lake, the mountains, the little towns, and the feeling of having nowhere urgent to be made this trip my personal S-tier experience.

My favorite kind of travel day is one with room to wander: take the ferry, get a little lost, stop for a coffee, and let the place set the pace. If I could give one piece of advice, it would be to leave some breathing room in the itinerary. The unplanned moments are often the ones that become the story.

Field note: Build in time for the journey between towns, not just the towns themselves. The in-between views are part of the destination.
April 15 through 19Morocco · S tier

Marrakesh, the Sahara, and a different kind of quiet

Morocco was one of the most memorable contrasts of the semester: busy streets and intricate details in Marrakesh, followed by the vastness of the Sahara. It felt immersive in a way that is difficult to describe until you experience the shift in landscape and rhythm yourself.

I booked through City Life Madrid, and our guide Adam was fantastic. If you are studying in Madrid and considering their Morocco trip, I would absolutely recommend looking into it. Go curious, stay flexible, and be present enough to notice the details beyond the photos.

Field note: For organized trips, read the itinerary carefully, check what is included, and pack for changing temperatures — desert evenings can feel very different from daytime.
February 26 – March 2England · A tier

London: a long weekend with big-city energy

London made the most of a long weekend: neighborhoods with distinct personalities, iconic landmarks, and enough cafés and streets to keep the days full without needing every hour planned. It was an easy city to build a trip around because you can mix the famous sights with ordinary wandering.

My approach for a short city break is to group activities by neighborhood, book any must-do items in advance, and leave open space for the unexpected. A packed itinerary can look efficient on paper and feel exhausting in real life.

February 20 through 22Portugal · B tier

Portugal: two cities, one weekend

Lisbon and Porto brought a different pace and atmosphere from Madrid. Splitting a weekend between cities is exciting, but it also means being thoughtful about transit time and what you actually want to experience. The best version of a multi-city trip is not trying to see absolutely everything; it is choosing a few moments to enjoy fully.

If you plan to combine cities, compare the time and cost of each leg before booking and remember that check-in, transfers, and getting to stations all count as travel time.

February 13 through 16Belgium + Netherlands

Brussels and Amsterdam: a split-weekend lesson

This weekend gave me two very different snapshots of Europe. Brussels landed lower on my personal tier list, while Amsterdam was a C tier for me — which is not a verdict on either city, just an honest record of how each trip felt to me in that moment. Weather, timing, expectations, and the people you travel with can all shape an experience.

My reminder to myself: a destination does not have to become a favorite to be worth visiting. Sometimes travel is about learning what you like, what you do not, and what you would do differently next time.

January through AprilSpain · Home base + day trips

Madrid, Segovia, and learning to stay a little longer

Madrid was more than the place I returned to between flights. It became the setting for the routines that made the semester feel like real life: coffee stops, favorite dinners, grocery runs, and familiar metro routes. Day trips to places such as Segovia gave me a way to explore Spain beyond the capital without turning every weekend into a major travel production.

Segovia is compact and walkable, with the Roman aqueduct and Alcázar making it especially rewarding for a day trip. Ávila and Toledo are also worth considering if you want medieval streets, history, and a change of pace from the city.
